# Theme

binnacle draws content in tones named by what it means, a glyph as a mark named by what it stands for, and a band in a background named by what the content it holds means; the theme gives each tone its colour, each mark its glyph and the tone it draws it in, and each background its colour. It says too how each kind of entry's folds start: how many rows each shows while folded, and whether it starts open. Markdown, the composer and the fullscreen's search are drawn in the same tones, as registrations change them.

A person changes any of it by asking an author, who registers the changes through the theme registration ([Authoring](authoring.md)); the author changes no view of their own for it, and binnacle draws and lays out every entry again in the theme as it stands.

## How it works

A view marks text, or a span within it, with a tone, never a colour ([ADR 11](../adr/0011-a-view-draws-with-blocks-in-the-themes-tones.md)), and the theme gives each tone its colour (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#tones`). A span may instead name a mark, and the theme draws the mark's glyph in the mark's tone, or in a tone the span names beside it (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#marks`). A band node names a background, as a view names a tone, and the theme fills every line of the band with it (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#backgrounds`); pi-tui's `Box` draws the padding and the fill (`pi:packages/tui/src/components/box.ts`). What the ui and the host draw themselves they draw with glyphs the theme holds too, beside the marks (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#chrome`); a view names none of them, so what the ui and the host draw is as much the theme's as a mark is.

A colour is one of the terminal's sixteen by name, which the person's palette decides; a 256-colour index; or an exact colour, in hex, `okhsl(…)` or `oklch(…)`, drawn in truecolor (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#Colour`). A theme may name colours once among its `vars` and give each by its name after. A theme may give a `light` and a `dark` variant, each laid over the rest on a terminal of that appearance (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#ThemeChanges`); the host turns on the terminal's light and dark reports, and a terminal turning light or dark draws everything again in the other variant, without a restart. An exact colour is drawn in truecolor, or as the nearest of 256 colours where the terminal has no more (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#TerminalLook`). A tone is a colour and the terminal's attributes, and it may carry a background of its own, as inline code needs (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#Style`).

Each part binnacle draws itself is drawn in pi's token for it, so a person recolours one part without the rest: an ask's frame in `border`, an approval's or a question's in `borderAccent`, and a show's gutter in `borderMuted`, the glyph that runs down beside what a show holds (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#chrome`), marking it as what the surface shows and did not write — a tool's output beside the transcript's own lines ([Tool cards](tool-cards.md)). A prompt's band is filled with the `userMessageBg` background and its words drawn in `userMessageText`, a steer's too, its line drawn with the `steer` mark in place of the prompt's ([Transcript](transcript.md)). A landed compaction's marker is drawn with the `compaction` mark in muted — one line where the model stopped seeing earlier history, its summary folded to nothing beneath ([Transcript](transcript.md)). A markdown block is drawn in the `md…` tokens (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#markdownTheme`), thinking in `thinkingText`, a tool card's title in `toolTitle` and its output in `toolOutput`, and the composer's frame in `borderMuted` and its select list in the tones (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#editorThemeOf`), and on the fullscreen a search's matches in `searchMatchText` on `searchMatchBg`, the current one bold as well (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#searchStylesOf`). The words a fold says of itself — what it holds, what it cut, what opening and folding it are called — are the theme's as its glyphs are (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#words`), so a person's theme can say them otherwise. So are the words an ask's bottom edge says of the keys the key table binds — `select` after the keys that invoke what has focus, `next` after the keys that move focus on, `page` after the keys that page an ask's prose, named only while it is paged — what each kind of offer does, `offer.<kind>`, said where an offer names no label of its own, and where the ask says it is: `offer.at` (`{count}/{of}`, the focused offer among all of them) on the bottom edge while its offers are windowed, and `page.at` (`page {count}/{of}`) on the row beneath the page shown. The last two are templates of two counts, `{count}` and `{of}`.

An ask's frame and a show's gutter are named by one word, `chrome.frame`: `rounded`, `square`, `heavy`, `double`, or `none`, their edges then blank. A border's pieces and the gutter beside it change one at a time (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#frames`). The room the layout leaves is the theme's too (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#Spacing`): a band's padding, an ask's padding inside its border, the room after a show's gutter, the indent of a call another call made, and the rows between entries; with how each kind's folds start, it is where a person makes the surface roomier or more compact. So is how every ask is given room (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#Asks`): `asks.rows` makes every ask a box of that height, edges included, however much it holds — padded inside when what it holds is shorter, its prose paged and its offers windowed when it is taller, never taller than the room its place gives it ([Blocks](blocks.md)). An ask may name its own rows, and keeps them whatever the theme says.

How a fold starts is the theme's, by the kind of entry it is drawn in (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/theme.ts#binnacleTheme`): read by the key its views are registered under — an entry kind, a quiet kind's dsh type, an authored fact's name — falling back to its kind when no start is given for its key. A fold that names its own rows keeps them, whatever the theme says; one that leaves them out takes its kind's start, or three rows. `open` starts a kind's folds open, and a person's toggle folds one back, as it opens one that starts folded. A placed screen has no entry kind, so its folds name their rows themselves: the [Trajectory](trajectory.md)'s do.

The files the agent presented are drawn under the `presented` mark in accent, and a workflow run under the `workflow` mark. A retry is drawn with the `retry` mark in muted, one line, the failure that caused it folded to nothing beneath ([Transcript](transcript.md)).

binnacle's own starts, a person's to change: the thinking fold, context, the fallback, authored facts, a compaction's summary and a retry's failure folded to nothing; a tool's and a result's output to three rows.

## A theme file in the profile

A theme is also a file: `<profile>/themes/<name>.json`, holding the changes of a theme registration as JSON — every part above, `vars` through `dark` — which an author agent writes and a person keeps, copies and changes ([ADR 17](../adr/0017-a-theme-is-a-file-drawn-as-soon-as-it-is-written.md)). Which files are used is the config of the built-in `binnacle-theme` row (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/cordis.patch.yml`), set in the profile's own patch as any row's is:

```yaml
- id: binnacle-theme
  config:
    theme: my-theme
    light: my-theme-light
    dark: my-theme-dark
```

`theme` names one file, which may hold its own variants; `light` and `dark` name one each, whose changes replace the theme file's own variant of that appearance (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/plugins/theme/index.ts`). A config naming no file registers nothing; a field that is not a file's name refuses the row where it loads, naming the field.

The file is checked where it is registered, and an author agent can check it before writing: against the JSON Schema the package ships, `binnacle:packages/binnacle/theme.schema.json`, draft-07, named in a file's `$schema`. The schema is a structural and lexical preflight — the parts, the field types, the enumerations, whole numbers — never refusing what the profile's own reading accepts; what only the reading can check stays the reading's: a var a colour names, what a new mark needs, a glyph's cell width. What the file holds that binnacle cannot draw is drawn as a notice naming its path and what to change, and the theme beneath it stays. The authoritative reading itself ships as a checker the package builds — `node <package>/dist/host/check-theme.js <file>` (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/host/check-theme.ts`) — reading the file the way the row does and printing `ok` or the reading's own message, so an agent knows what the surface will accept before it writes into the profile.

**dusk** ships as the worked example, beside the author skill's theme chapter (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/skills/binnacle-author/themes/dusk.json`): the look binnacle's predecessor shipped — a night-plum ground under neon orchid — as one file holding its light twin, every colour of it theme data alone, no view changed to draw it. An author agent asked for “a theme like dusk” finds the file there.

A pi theme file works as it is (`pi:packages/coding-agent/docs/themes.md`): every top-level field of pi's theme schema is either read — `vars` as the theme's own, `colors` as tones and backgrounds — or ignored: `$schema`, `name`, `export`, `appearance`. In `colors`, a token ending in `Bg` fills that background and every other token draws as that tone's colour; `""`, the terminal's own, leaves the token as binnacle has it; where the file's own `tones` or `backgrounds` name the same token, theirs wins.

The host reads and watches the files through a named grant, `themeFile` ([Authoring](authoring.md)): a file written again is checked and registered again, and the redraw that already exists does the rest — no restart. A file removed, or text that is not JSON, is drawn as a notice naming its path while the last theme stays. On the main screen, rows already printed keep their theme ([ADR 14](../adr/0014-on-the-main-screen-a-printed-row-never-changes.md)): the alternate screen and what is drawn again change; the scrollback does not.

## Choices

- The tones are pi's names for what content means (`pi:packages/coding-agent/docs/themes.md`).
- binnacle's own colours follow the terminal's: where it reports its background, each tone and background takes its hue from one of the terminal's sixteen, at a lightness that keeps its contrast on that background, light or dark (`binnacle:packages/binnacle/src/ui/derived-colours.ts#deriveColours`), asked again when the terminal turns light or dark. An author's theme lays over them.
- Where the terminal reports nothing, each tone and background is one of its own sixteen, so the person's palette decides how it looks, as it does for everything else they run; the prompt band is filled with the terminal's bright black, the grey a band reads as on a light palette and a dark one alike.
- An ask is framed `rounded`; a band is padded by one, an ask by one column inside its border, a show's gutter one column from what it holds, a call another call made indented by two columns, and entries one row apart. No `asks.rows` is given out of the box: an ask grows with what it holds, up to its room.
- Each mark's glyph is one column wide and a glyph every terminal's fonts carry, so a mark never misaligns what it stands before.
- Which marks exist, the glyph each draws and the tone it draws it in, change on request; so do the tones and the backgrounds, and the colour each takes.
- How each kind's folds start — how many rows each shows, and whether it starts open — changes on request the same way; binnacle's own starts are those above.
- A fenced code block is not highlighted, so its lines are the terminal's own.
- The frames — `border`, `borderMuted` — are dim, as chrome nobody reads, and `borderAccent` is the accent's cyan, for an ask that waits on the person. Ordinary prose, a markdown answer's and a view's untoned text, is drawn in `text`, the terminal's own foreground. In markdown, inline code is yellow, a link and a bullet cyan, a heading bold, a quote, a rule and a link's address dim, and a code block the terminal's own; thinking is dim; a prompt's band bright black, and its words, a card's title and its output the terminal's own.
- An ask's bottom edge says `select`, `next` and — while its prose is paged — `page` after the keys, and where it is: `7/20` while its offers are windowed, in the theme's `offer.at`; the row beneath the page shown says `page 2/5`, in `page.at`. An offer that names no label says its kind: `expand`, `choose`, `open`, `copy`, `answer`, `allow` for a grant, `dismiss`.
